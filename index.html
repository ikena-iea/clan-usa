How scoring works

Two separate scores. Clan score is about contribution and is the basis for staying in the clan. War score is about war only and affects whether you get picked for the next war. They are independent: not warring does not hurt your clan score, and a great war record does not cover for contributing nothing.

Legendary 85+ Epic 70 to 84 Rare 55 to 69 Uncommon 40 to 54 Common below 40

Tiers are fixed targets, not a ranking. Everyone can be Legendary at the same time. Nobody drops because someone else improved.

Clan score = 40% donations, 35% raids, 25% capital contributions.
Donations counts troops given only, never what you receive. Raids is attacks used out of attacks available across recent weekends. Capital contributions is gold given compared to gold you looted, so it measures whether you spend what you earn rather than how much you raid.

War score = 60% reliability, 40% performance.
Reliability is attacks used out of attacks you were rostered for. Performance scores each attack as (stars + destruction/100) × TH multiplier, where the multiplier is 1 + 0.3 × (defender TH − attacker TH), capped between 0.4 and 1.8.

That means a perfect 3-star at 100% against an equal Town Hall is worth 4.0 points, the benchmark. Attacking up is worth more, and farming a much lower Town Hall is worth much less, so clearing easy bases will not carry your score.

Caps. Two or more misses in your last 10 rostered attacks caps your war tier at Rare. Three or more caps it at Uncommon. A performance score under 50, which is the consistent-farming range, also caps at Rare regardless of how reliable you are.

New members are not scored for the first 14 days. Pillars still show so you can see data accumulating, but no tier is assigned until there is enough history to be fair about it.
